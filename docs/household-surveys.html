03 Comparable microdata across destinations

Household surveys: the IDB harmonized bank, Spain's EPA and the US surveys

Three families of household surveys share one concept, a person born in another country, and the same way of measuring it: an expansion-weighted microdata record with age, sex, education, work and income. That is what makes it possible to ask the same question of a Colombian in Santiago, in Madrid and in Miami. This page profiles the three families together and sends you to page 01 for the United States in depth.

One concept, three families

In the IDB harmonized bank the variable migrante_ci flags a person born in another country. Spain's EPA records the country of birth and the US ACS and CPS record the detailed birthplace. All three therefore measure the first generation, independently of citizenship or legal status, and all three let the same indicators (employment, participation, education, income, household composition) be computed for migrants and for natives with the same code. What differs is depth: the US surveys add year of arrival and naturalization, the Spanish extract adds years of residence, and the LAC bank only knows whether a migrant has been in the country five years or more.

Profile

IDB harmonized household surveys (Banco de Datos Armonizado de Encuestas de Hogares)

The IDB Social Sector harmonizes the national labour-force and living-standards surveys of 26 countries into one variable dictionary, one file per country, year and round, and computes 234 indicators from them, every one breakable by migrant status.

Producer
IDB Social Sector (SCL), from the surveys of national statistical offices: EPH and EPHC (Argentina), PNAD and PNADC (Brazil), CASEN (Chile), GEIH (Colombia), ENAHO (Costa Rica and Peru), ENCFT (Dominican Republic), ENEMDU (Ecuador), EHPM (El Salvador), ENCOVI and ENEI (Guatemala), EPHPM (Honduras), ENIGH (Mexico), ECH (Uruguay), labour force surveys in the Caribbean, among others.
Unit of record
A person inside a household, with the survey's expansion factor (factor_ci) and household identifiers.
Geography
National, urban and rural; regions where the survey allows.
Time coverage
1986–2025. The inventory lists 567 harmonized country-year rounds up to 2022 (January 2025 snapshot); 2023–2025 rounds exist for Argentina, Chile, Colombia, Costa Rica, Dominican Republic, Ecuador, Guatemala, Paraguay, Peru, El Salvador and Uruguay.
Periodicity
Annual for most countries; quarterly or semestral rounds for Argentina, Colombia, Paraguay, the Dominican Republic and others.
Migration variables
migrante_ci (born abroad), migrantiguo5_ci (migrant with five years or more in the country), miglac_ci (born in a LAC country). Country of birth (mig_pais_ci) only in the HDMF project files for Colombia, Ecuador and Peru.
Other variables
Demography, education (levels, attendance, years), labour (participation, employment, informality, occupation, sector, hours, contracts, earnings), income and poverty (harmonized lines), housing and water, social protection, ethnicity and disability where asked.
What it does not have
Year of arrival; citizenship or legal status; country of birth in most rounds; migrants in countries whose survey omits the birthplace question (Mexico's ENIGH 2022 and Paraguay's EPHC 2023 carry no flag in our combined file).
Access
Microdata on the IDB SURVEYS share with an access right per round (public or restricted). Harmonized indicators are disseminated through the IDB's data portals. Code is public on GitHub (EL-BID).
Sample sizes
Migrants are a small share in most countries (Colombia 4.9%, Argentina 4.9%, Dominican Republic 5.8%, Ecuador 1.5%, Peru 0.8%, Guatemala 0.6% in 2024), so single-year migrant estimates carry wide confidence intervals outside the big host countries.
Existing products
A ten-survey combined file 2022–2023 with migrant status, age, sex and employment; 2024 indicator runs for six countries; the Settling In Latin America 2021 tables (natives vs foreign-born across 15 indicators) from the How do Migrants Fare project.

Profile

Spain: Encuesta de Población Activa (EPA), harmonized by the HDMF project

Spain hosts the largest LAC diaspora in Europe. The EPA is INE's quarterly labour-force survey; the How do Migrants Fare in LAC project harmonized it with the same variable names as the LAC bank, and a special request to INE adds the country of birth that the public file replaces with a region.

Producer
Instituto Nacional de Estadística (INE); harmonized by the IDB project How do Migrants Fare in LAC (HDMF).
Unit of record
A person inside a household; quarterly weights (an annual file stacks four quarters, so the annual weight is the quarterly factor divided by four).
Time coverage
2014 public microdata (region of birth only) and 2018–2025 special request with country of birth.
Periodicity
Quarterly, used as annual stacks.
Migration variables
Country of birth (paina1), nationality (nac1), years of residence in Spain (anore).
Other variables
Age, sex, employment status, hours, part-time, temporary contracts, sector, occupation (ISCO), education, household type.
Sample
About 1.09 million LAC-born residents aged 16 and over in 2024 (weighted), from 27,000 person-quarter records; roughly 6,300 distinct people a year because households stay six quarters.
Caveats
Standard errors are inflated by the panel overlap (factor 6T/(T+5)); the public 2014 file has no country detail; two harmonization bugs are documented (household relationship labels and temporary contracts).
Existing products
The Spain block of the Age Shift deck (pyramids, employment rates, by-country detail 2018–2024) and the Spain chapter of the Mexican diaspora draft.

Profile

United States: ACS and CPS (see page 01)

Role here
The ACS is the US household survey; the CPS adds the second generation. Both measure country of birth directly.
Comparable indicators
Employment, participation, education and income by origin, age and sex, with the same age groups (five-year bins, 65+ open) and employment definition as the Spain block of the Age Shift deck.
Where to read more
Page 01 holds the full profile, the two charts and the traps.

Questions it answers

  • What share of each country's population was born abroad, and how it has moved over three decades of rounds.
  • Whether migrants work more or less than natives, in which sectors, with which contracts and hours, and at what pay, with the same definitions in every country.
  • Education and overqualification of migrants relative to natives; school attendance of migrant children.
  • Poverty and income of migrant households against harmonized poverty lines.
  • For Spain and the US: the same indicators by country of birth, years of residence and age, to compare the two main destinations outside the region.

Known traps

Small migrant samples

Below one or two percent of the population, a single round gives a few hundred migrant records. Pool rounds or report confidence intervals; the indicator files carry standard errors and a quality flag.

Not every survey asks where you were born

Mexico's ENIGH and some Paraguayan rounds have no usable birthplace question; the harmonized flag is missing rather than zero.

No year of arrival in the bank

Only the five-years-or-more flag exists. Spain's EPA and the US surveys have years of residence and year of immigration.

Employment definitions are national

condocup_ci follows each country's official definition of employment; cross-country gaps partly reflect that.

Panel surveys inflate precision

The EPA reinterviews households for six quarters with no identifier to link them, so naive standard errors are about four times too small.

Access rights differ by round

Some rounds are public, others restricted; the inventory flags each. Aggregates like the ones here can be shared, microdata cannot.
