02 Europe as destination

Eurostat migration statistics, with the companion sources of the European project

Eurostat compiles what 31 national statistical institutes and interior ministries report under the EU migration statistics regulation: residents by country of birth and citizenship, residence permits by reason, asylum applications and decisions, naturalisations, and enforcement. It is the only source that follows legal pathways and status changes, and it is all aggregate: no person-level records.

Profile

Eurostat migration and asylum statistics (the migr_ datasets)

We keep 118 datasets in which LAC citizens or LAC-born people can be identified, downloaded in full through the Eurostat dissemination API and stored as tidy tables with every code and label. A small Python client pins each dataset to its totals and to the European reporting countries, so a series for any origin is one call.

Producer
Eurostat, from national statistical institutes and interior ministries under Regulation (EC) 862/2007.
Unit of record
Aggregate counts: persons (population, flows, naturalisations), permits, applications, decisions, enforcement events. No microdata.
Geography
Reporting countries: EU27, EFTA (Switzerland, Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway), the United Kingdom to 2019, and candidate countries for some series. Origin: every country, by citizenship (most datasets) or country of birth (five datasets).
Time coverage
Population by country of birth and citizenship 1998–2025; residence permits 2008–2025; asylum 2008–2026; enforcement 2008–2025; citizenship acquisitions 1998–2024.
Periodicity
Annual; asylum applications and pending cases also monthly.
Dimensions
Citizenship or country of birth; age; sex; permit reason (family, education, employment, other) and length; permit type (Blue Card, intra-corporate transfer, study, seasonal work); applicant type (first-time, subsequent); decision; enforcement type (overstay vs illegal entry, return type and destination).
What it does not have
Socioeconomic detail by origin (the labour-force survey tables cannot single out LAC countries); France and Germany in the born-in population series; the UK after 2019; sub-national geography.
Access and licence
Open data, Creative Commons BY 4.0 with attribution; API and bulk download, revised continuously.
What we hold
118 datasets as tidy parquet (1.2 billion rows, 262 MB), complete and not filtered to any origin, refreshed September 2026; the metadata catalogue and code lists; GISCO boundaries for maps.
Existing products
A book draft on the Mexican diaspora outside the United States (three versions, 142 charts), a Europe-wide series 1998–2025 (stock, immigration, emigration, naturalisations), a source comparison against Gaskin and Abel and UN DESA, and 2024 return tables for Northern Central America.

Profile

Companion sources kept in the same project

IME consular records
Instituto de los Mexicanos en el Exterior: fact sheets for 159 countries (2025) and an 11-country panel 2018–2025 of registered Mexicans by sex, mean age, children, occupation, civil status, dual nationality and state of origin. Some years repeat the previous year's figures and are flagged.
Spain EPA extract (HDMF)
Person-level microdata of Mexico-born residents 2018–2025 and comparison indicators for natives and all migrants, drawn from the IDB's How do Migrants Fare project. Profiled on page 03.
Gaskin and Abel (2025) stocks
Modelled stock of people born in Mexico in each of 230 destinations, 1990–2024, used to benchmark Eurostat (agreement within 2–3% when the born-in series is used). Profiled on page 04.
Validation
Spain 2024, Mexico-born: Eurostat 79,581, Gaskin and Abel 77,984, UN DESA 77,724. Permit series read 76% lower than any of these because permits are not people.

Questions it answers

  • How many people born in, or holding the citizenship of, each LAC country live in each European country, and since when.
  • Through which legal door they arrive: family, study, work or protection, and whether study permits turn into work permits.
  • How many ask for asylum each month, by citizenship, and how their cases are decided (recognition rates, pending stock).
  • How many naturalize, which quietly removes them from every citizenship-based series.
  • Irregular stay (overstay vs illegal entry), orders to leave and returns, by citizenship and destination.

Known traps

Three definitions of a national

citizen = passport holder; born in = first generation whatever the passport; previous residence = anyone who moved from that country. They answer different questions and give different numbers. Use country of birth for a stable diaspora measure and citizenship for legal-status questions.

Geo aggregates sit next to countries

EU27_2020 and EA20 are rows in the same column as DE or ES. Summing naively doubles the total; keep two-letter reporting countries only.

Totals next to components

Every dimension carries TOTAL beside its parts (age, sex, reason). Pick the total or the parts, never both.

Two age definitions double flows

Immigration, emigration and naturalisation datasets repeat totals under two age definitions (COMPLET and REACH). Pin one.

Permits are not people

Stocks of valid permits exclude naturalized citizens and EU-citizen family members; first permits count documents, not arrivals. The born-in population series is the right denominator.

Reporting countries change

First permits were reported by 16 countries in 2010 and 31 in 2025; Germany only from 2019. Trends need a fixed country panel.

Suppression and revisions

Small counts are rounded (returns data to 5) or suppressed; OBS_FLAG carries confidentiality and break-in-series markers. Figures are revised; note the download date.

France and Germany are missing by birth

Neither reports population by country of birth, which is why the stock chart on this page understates the LAC-born total in Europe.
