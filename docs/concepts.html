Reading across sources

Concepts and comparability

Every source on this site counts something slightly different: a person born abroad, a passport, a permit, an application, an event. Most disagreements between numbers are definitions, dates or universes rather than errors. This page lists the ones we meet most often and puts the five families side by side.

The same number from different sources

Three examples from our own work, each with the two or three figures that circulate.

  • People born in Mexico living in the United States, 2024: 11.59 million by IPUMS birthplace (any citizenship) and 11.15 million as Census foreign-born (naturalized or non-citizen); the gap is people born abroad to US-citizen parents.
  • People born in Mexico living in Spain, 2024: Eurostat 79,581; Gaskin and Abel 77,984; UN DESA 77,724; Mexican citizens with a valid permit 33,849. The first three agree within 3%; the permit count answers a different question.
  • Immigrants living in Latin America and the Caribbean, 2024: 17.5 million in UN DESA's regional row, 16.6 million when the 26 IDB borrowers are summed, 13.3 million intra-regional migrants in FLown's 1 January estimate for the 33-country region.

Reading our charts

  • Every chart has a table view, its CSV and a PNG in light and dark versions for slides.
  • Series keep their colour across the site; grey always marks the residual group (other countries, non-LAC citizens).
  • Partial periods are hatched; modelled series say so in the subtitle; notes under the chart carry the caveat that matters most.
  • The headline numbers behind each chart are checked against the source project's documentation each time the site is rebuilt.
