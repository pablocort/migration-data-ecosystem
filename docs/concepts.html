Reading across sources

Concepts: why numbers differ

Each source counts something different, such as a person born abroad, a passport, a permit or an event. Most gaps between numbers come from that, not from errors.

Four ways to count a migrant

What each source counts, and who it leaves out.

ConceptWhat is countedUsed byLeaves out
Born abroadPeople born in another country, whatever their passportUS surveys, household surveys, UN DESA, Eurostat by birthChildren of migrants born in the destination
CitizenshipPeople with a foreign passportEurostat permits and asylum, ICE removalsMigrants who naturalized
Permit or applicationDocuments, not peopleEurostat permits, TPS and DACA applicationsPeople without papers. One person can hold several documents.
EventSomething that happened, such as a removal, an arrest or a detentionDeportation Data Project, Eurostat returnsNothing, but events repeat for the same person

The same number from different sources

  • People born in Mexico living in the US, 2024: 11.6 million by place of birth and 11.2 million as foreign-born. The gap is people born abroad to US parents.
  • People born in Mexico living in Spain: about 80,000 in 2024, by Eurostat, the UN and the annual estimates. Only 34,000 Mexicans held a valid permit in 2025.
  • Migrants living in LAC, 2024: 17.5 million in the UN regional total and 16.6 million in the 26 IDB borrowing countries.
