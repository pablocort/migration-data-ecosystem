01 The United States as a destination

United States: census and survey data

Person-level data from the US Census Bureau, harmonized by IPUMS. It is our richest view of the 25.8 million people born in LAC who live in the US.

Profile

American Community Survey and US census (IPUMS USA)

The largest US household survey. About 3.5 million addresses a year answer questions on birthplace, citizenship, work and income.

What it is
The US household survey, plus the 1980, 1990 and 2000 censuses, in one series.
Who produces it
US Census Bureau. IPUMS, at the University of Minnesota, harmonizes it.
Unit
People in sampled households. Weights turn the sample into population totals.
Coverage
The United States, 1980–2024. About 1% of the population each year.
How often
Every year since 2001. The 2020 data are experimental.
Migration variables
Country of birth, year of arrival, citizenship, year of naturalization and residence a year ago.
Best for
The size and profile of each diaspora: age, work, income and time in the US.
What we have built with it
Diaspora profiles by country of birth. A study of how the LAC-born population aged from 2014 to 2024.
Access
Free and public, with registration. Cite IPUMS.

Profile

Current Population Survey (IPUMS CPS)

A smaller survey. Its yearly supplement asks where each person's parents were born.

What it is
The US labour force survey and its yearly income supplement.
Who produces it
US Census Bureau and Bureau of Labor Statistics. IPUMS harmonizes it.
Unit
People in sampled households.
Coverage
The United States, 1994–2025.
How often
Every year, in March.
Migration variables
Country of birth, year of arrival, citizenship and the parents' country of birth.
Best for
The second generation: people born in the US to migrant parents.
What we have built with it
Series of first and second generation migrants, for example from Venezuela.
Access
Free and public, with registration. Cite IPUMS.

Questions it answers

  • How many people from each LAC country live in the US, and how that changed since 1980.
  • How old they are, how long they have been in the US, and whether they naturalized.
  • Where they work and how much they earn, by origin, age and sex.
  • How many children of migrants were born in the US.

Be careful with

Two definitions of foreign-born

The Census leaves out people born abroad to US-citizen parents. Counts by place of birth include them and run about 4% higher.

Panama

People born in the former Canal Zone to US parents count as born in Panama. That inflates the Panamanian figure.

2020

The pandemic cut survey responses in 2020. Treat that year with care.

Small groups

Small origins have few survey records. Pool years or report margins of error.

Pairs well with

  • 03 Household surveys: the same definition of migrant, in LAC and Spain.
  • 04 Global estimates: the US numbers next to UN and modelled estimates.
  • 05 Deportation data: removals count citizenship, not birth. Compare with care.
