01 United States as destination

ACS and CPS microdata: IPUMS USA, Census PUMS and IPUMS CPS

Person-level survey microdata from the US Census Bureau, harmonized and distributed by IPUMS. It is the most detailed view we have of the 25.8 million people born in Latin America and the Caribbean who live in the United States: who they are, when they arrived, whether they naturalized, what they earn and where they work.

Profile

IPUMS USA: decennial census and American Community Survey microdata

The ACS is the household survey of the United States: about 3.5 million addresses a year, asked about birthplace, citizenship, year of entry, education, work and income. IPUMS harmonizes it with the decennial censuses, so one extract spans 1980 to 2024. The Census Bureau publishes the same records as the Public Use Microdata Sample (PUMS), reachable through its API; the charts below use that route.

Producer
US Census Bureau; harmonized and distributed by IPUMS USA (University of Minnesota).
Unit of record
A person inside a sampled household, with a person weight (PERWT in IPUMS, PWGTP in PUMS). Every figure is weighted.
Geography
United States, national level in our work. State, county and PUMA are available; a separate geographic extract (2000–2024) holds them.
Time coverage
Census 1980, 1990, 2000 (5% samples) and ACS 2001–2024 (1-year, about 1% of the population). Our main extract covers 2014–2024.
Periodicity
Annual. ACS 2020 used experimental weights and is not fully comparable.
Sample size
About 3.2 million persons per ACS year; roughly 250,000 LAC-born records per year, enough for single countries of birth down to about 100,000 people.
Migration variables
Detailed country of birth (BPLD), citizenship status (CITIZEN), year of naturalization (YRNATUR), year of immigration (YRIMMIG), years in the US (YRSUSA1), residence one year ago including abroad (MIGRATE1, MIGPLAC1), ancestry, Hispanic origin, language and English ability.
Other variables
Age, sex, relationship to head, education, school attendance, degree field, employment status, occupation, industry, weeks and hours worked, total, wage and Social Security income, poverty status, household income.
What it does not have
Legal status beyond citizenship; parents' birthplace (use the CPS); remittances (we join central-bank data instead).
Access and licence
Free, public; registration at usa.ipums.org. The Census API serves the PUMS with a free key. Microdata are never redistributed; aggregates may be published with citation.
What we hold
A 2014–2024 extract with 54 variables (6.8 GB), a 1980–2023 extract, the full ACS 2024, a geographic extract 2000–2024, and a compact PUMS cache of all LAC-born persons 2014–2024 (the input of the charts here).
Existing products
The 2014–2024 Age Shift deck for 11 countries (US and Spain), an annual diaspora panel 2014–2024, population by country 1980–2024, years-of-residence distributions, a Venezuelan first and second generation series 1980–2025.

Profile

IPUMS CPS: Current Population Survey, Annual Social and Economic Supplement

The March supplement of the CPS is smaller than the ACS but asks about the parents' country of birth, which makes it the only survey source for the second generation.

Producer
US Bureau of Labor Statistics and Census Bureau; harmonized by IPUMS CPS.
Unit of record
A person inside a sampled household; ASEC supplement weights.
Time coverage
Country of birth from 1994; our extracts cover 1994–2025 and 2014–2025.
Periodicity
Annual (March).
Sample size
About 90,000 households; small for origins other than Mexico, so multi-year pooling is usual.
Migration variables
Country of birth (PENATVTY), year of entry in intervals (PEINUSYR), citizenship (PRCITSHP), mother's and father's country of birth (PEMNTVTY, PEFNTVTY).
Strengths
Second generation; a long annual series; detailed income and programme participation.
Caveats
Year of entry is grouped; sampling error is large for single countries; weights differ from the ACS so levels are not identical.

Questions it answers

  • How many people born in each LAC country live in the United States, and how that stock changed year by year since 1980.
  • How the diaspora is ageing: age structure, mean age, the shift from the 18–29 to the 60+ group, and how uneven it is across origins (Mexico ages fast, Honduras gets younger).
  • How long people have been in the country (years since arrival), which matters because remittances grow over the first fifteen years abroad.
  • Who naturalized and when; who arrived in the last year (residence one year ago abroad).
  • Employment, occupation, industry, earnings and total income by origin, sex and age, in constant dollars.
  • Children of migrants (second generation) through the CPS parents' birthplace.

Known traps

Foreign-born is not born-in

Census 'foreign-born' (naturalized or non-citizen) leaves out people born abroad to US-citizen parents. IPUMS birthplace counts include them, so Mexico reads 11.6 million by birthplace and 11.2 million as foreign-born in 2024. Say which one you use.

Panama is overcounted

The IPUMS birthplace code for Panama includes US citizens born in the Canal Zone. Expect 30–50% more than Census table B05006.

2020 is experimental

The 2020 ACS 1-year release uses experimental weights because of pandemic non-response. Treat 2020 as a dotted point.

Two file conventions

Older extracts are pipe-separated with text labels (bpld == 'Mexico'); newer ones use numeric codes (BPLD == 20000). Scripts that mix them need a mapping.

Not-in-universe codes

INCTOT uses 9999999 for not applicable and INCWAGE uses 999999. Average either without filtering and the means explode.

Geography needs a merge

The main extract has no state. Merge the geographic extract on year, sample, household serial and person number before mapping.

Hours worked are missing in the IPUMS extract

The PUMS cache from the Census API fills WKHP (usual hours) for the full-time and part-time split; records and weights match IPUMS one to one.

A mislabelled Mexico file

One 2024 PUMS download used place-of-birth code 210 (India) instead of 303 (Mexico). Do not reuse the curated Mexico 2024 rows.
