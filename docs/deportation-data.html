05 US immigration enforcement, record by record

Deportation Data Project: ICE, USCIS and CBP records

The Deportation Data Project obtains individual-level records from US immigration agencies through Freedom of Information Act requests and publishes them with documentation. We hold ICE removals, arrests and detentions from fiscal year 2012 to March 2026, every TPS and DACA application since 2000, and partial Border Patrol encounter files. It is the only source that sees enforcement one person, one event at a time.

Profile

ICE removals, arrests and detentions

Two FOIA releases (2023-ICFO-42034 and 2026-ICLI-00005) cover fiscal years 2012 to 2026 and overlap in FY2023. Our pipeline reads the spreadsheets, reconciles column names, keeps the newer batch for FY2023, stacks removals with arrests that carry a final order, and collapses detention stints into continuous stays.

Producer
US Immigration and Customs Enforcement records released under FOIA; compiled, cleaned and documented by the Deportation Data Project (deportationdata.org).
Unit of record
One row per removal, per administrative arrest, or per detention stint (a facility book-in), with an anonymized person identifier. Stints are collapsed to stays: 11.1 million stints become 4.8 million stays of 4.1 million people.
Geography
United States; departure country and port; area of responsibility, state and county in the newer batch (state is filled for only 9% of FY2024 removals).
Time coverage
FY2012 to 11 March 2026 (FY2026 partial). Citizenship and birth country in detention records only from FY2023.
Periodicity
Daily records, used by fiscal year (October to September) and by month.
Variables
Citizenship country, birth country, gender, birth year, entry status, entry date, apprehension date, departure date, country and port, case category and status, final order, criminality (charges, threat level, convictions), arresting agency, programme, prior deportation, gang or terrorist flags; detention: facility, book-in and book-out dates, release reason, classification, bond.
Size
3.8 million removal rows (3.68 million after deduplication), 4.2 million enforcement rows, 6.3 GB of detention records.
Strengths
Monthly series by citizenship since October 2011; third-country removals (departure country different from citizenship); time since last entry; the profile shift of 2025 (interior arrests, convictions, time in the country).
Access
Public download with documentation; citation to the Deportation Data Project required.
Existing products
Removals by fiscal year for the 26 IDB countries (3.5 million, 95.5% of all removals), by sex and age, removals to third countries, mean and median age by citizenship and by destination, time in the US before removal, the 2024-vs-2025 profile, a monthly series for El Salvador, and a map of removals aged 40 and over by state.

Profile

USCIS Temporary Protected Status and DACA applications

Application-level records of the two main discretionary protections, released in Mukherjee v. USCIS and published by the project in August 2026. A pending TPS stock of 1.16 million applications in February 2026 is visible nowhere else at this frequency.

Producer
US Citizenship and Immigration Services; compiled by the Deportation Data Project.
Unit of record
One row per application (form I-821 for TPS, I-821D for DACA), not per person: 11.2 million rows, 6.7 million TPS and 4.5 million DACA.
Time coverage
January 2000 to 11 February 2026 (DACA from August 2012).
Periodicity
Daily receipt and decision dates, used monthly.
Variables
Receipt date, decision date and outcome, form type, initial or renewal, country of birth, sex, age group.
Strengths
Backlogs and decision times by month; Haitian TPS stock reconstructed (290,000 probably in force vs 353,000 in DHS counts).
Caveats
Pending applications carry a placeholder decision date; the DACA form-type field is contaminated (use the filled-in version); receipts and decisions run on different clocks; USCIS splits by birth country while ICE splits by citizenship.

Profile

CBP Border Patrol encounters

Unit of record
One row per encounter (apprehension) by the US Border Patrol.
Time coverage
FY2016–FY2018 and FY2023–FY2024; FY2019–FY2022 missing.
Status
Only Honduras has been processed locally; provenance of the files is not documented in the folder. For encounter totals we use DHS OHSS published tables instead.

Questions it answers

  • How many citizens of each LAC country ICE removed each month and fiscal year since 2012, and what share of all removals LAC represents (92% in FY2025).
  • Who is being removed: sex, age, time since last entry, criminal history, arresting agency (border vs interior), prior deportations, and how that profile changed between 2024 and 2025.
  • Removals to a third country, from 4,500 in FY2023 to 36,700 in FY2025, by citizenship and destination.
  • Detention: how long people are held, where, and whether the stay ends in removal.
  • TPS and DACA: applications filed and decided per month, pending stock, decision times, by country of birth.

Known traps

FY2023 appears twice

Both FOIA batches cover FY2023. Tables built before April 2026 sum them and show 295,450 instead of 151,819. Use only deduplicated tables.

Fiscal, not calendar

FY2025 runs from October 2024 to September 2025. Calendar-year cuts exist for the age tables and the profile analysis; the two are not comparable column by column.

FY2026 is a stub

Records stop on 11 March 2026. Hatched in the chart; never read as a drop.

Entry date is the last entry

Time in the US before removal measures time since the last recorded entry, with a median of 33 days for border cases. It is not length of residence.

Removals are not all departures

CBP returns and Title 42 expulsions (FY2020–FY2022) are not in these files; those years look empty for that reason.

Four Caribbean spellings

ICE writes St. Lucia, St. Vincent-Grenadines, Antigua-Barbuda and St. Kitts-Nevis in a form the country lists miss; 785 removals vanish from most LAC tables.

Age is approximate

Birth dates are redacted; age is the year minus the birth year, so fiscal-year ages run up to one year high for October to December departures.

Reliability warning

The project itself flagged concerns about the March 2026 removals release. Compare with ICE's own annual report before publishing a 2026 figure.

How to cite the data

Deportation Data Project (deportationdata.org): ICE removals, arrests and detentions (FOIA 2023-ICFO-42034 and 2026-ICLI-00005); USCIS TPS and DACA application data released in Mukherjee v. USCIS; CBP Border Patrol encounter data. Data accessed March and August 2026.
