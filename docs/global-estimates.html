04 Global anchors for stocks, flows, displacement and remittances

UN DESA and global estimates

The folder we call undesa holds five different products. UN DESA's International Migrant Stock is the official anchor; FLown adds an annual, modelled bilateral series; UNHCR adds forced displacement; the World Population Prospects give denominators; and the remittance files tie the diaspora to the money it sends home. This page classifies them and shows one chart per product.

Profile

UN DESA International Migrant Stock 2024

The United Nations estimate of how many international migrants live in every country, by country of origin and sex, every five years since 1990 and again in 2024. It is the reference everyone else is benchmarked against, including Eurostat and the modelled series below.

Producer
UN DESA Population Division, International Migrant Stock 2024 (and the 2020 revision for age).
Unit of record
Migrant stock in persons at mid-year, by destination, origin and sex; a coverage code per row says whether the figure counts the foreign-born, foreign citizens, includes refugees or is imputed.
Geography
Around 230 countries and areas plus regional and development-group aggregates; the LAC aggregate covers 33 countries and territories, while the 26 IDB borrowers hold 95% of its immigrants.
Time coverage
1990, 1995, 2000, 2005, 2010, 2015, 2020, 2024. Age and sex by destination only in the 2020 revision (no 2024 age data).
Periodicity
Five-year steps, then 2024.
Strengths
Official, comparable, bilateral; the right anchor for shares and levels.
Caveats
Sparse in time; imputations between censuses; name variants (Bolivia (Plurinational State of), Venezuela (Bolivarian Republic of), United States of America with an asterisk); the 2024 file is a new vintage, not an extension of the 2020 series.
Access
Open, with attribution.
What we hold
The 2024 and 2020 bilateral files, the 2020 age file, a merged working table (destination by origin by year), long-format exports and the canonical destination and origin tables, some of which carry manual edits.

Profile

FLown annual bilateral estimates (Azose and Raftery 2019, via Gaskin and Abel 2025)

A statistical model that turns the UN stocks, population data and other inputs into an annual bilateral series of stocks and flows with uncertainty. It is the only source that lets us see year by year where the diaspora of each country lives and how fast each corridor moves.

Producer
Azose and Raftery (2019) FLown estimates, distributed with Gaskin and Abel (2025), Deep learning four decades of human migration (arXiv 2506.22821; dataset on Hugging Face, GPL-3.0).
Unit of record
Modelled bilateral stock at 1 January and flows by previous residence and by birth, with standard deviations; country totals of immigration, emigration and net migration.
Geography
All countries (ISO3), including LAC territories.
Time coverage
Annual, 1990–2024.
Strengths
Annual and bilateral; splits every diaspora by destination (LAC, United States, Spain, other); captures the Venezuelan displacement inside the region (Venezuela explains 81% of the 2020–2024 rise in intra-LAC stock).
Caveats
Model output, not counts; two versions circulate and differ by up to 65% on small corridors (we use the LSE file); stocks are dated 1 January, UN DESA's mid-year; the model concentrates 71.5% of the 2020–2024 growth of the US stock in 2023; a few tiny corridors go negative; the unilateral file's population columns repeat the flows.
What we hold
The bilateral and unilateral CSVs, intra-LAC panels, the emigrants-by-destination table (48 origins, 1990–2024) and the eligibility workbook for the IDB migration facility.

Profile

UNHCR Refugee Data Finder

Persons under UNHCR's mandate by country of origin and country of asylum: refugees, asylum-seekers, returned refugees, internally displaced, stateless, others of concern and, since 2018, other people in need of international protection, the category that holds most displaced Venezuelans.

Producer
UNHCR, population statistics (API v1).
Unit of record
End-year stock of persons by population type, origin and asylum country.
Geography
LAC countries as origin (26 origins, 105 host countries) and as asylum (26 hosts, 174 origins).
Time coverage
1990–2024, annual.
Strengths
The forced-displacement layer; bilateral; consistent definitions across hosts.
Caveats
Asylum-seeker stocks depend on processing backlogs, not only on arrivals; displaced Venezuelans are mostly counted as other people in need of protection (5.9 million in 2024) rather than refugees; small numbers before 2000 reflect reporting, not peace.
Access
Open, with attribution.

Profile

Remittances: KNOMAD and World Bank, central-bank corridors, remittance prices

Three layers. Macro inflows per country from KNOMAD and the World Bank; bilateral inflows by sending country compiled from central banks for fifteen recipients; and the cost of sending money, from the World Bank's Remittance Prices Worldwide. A derived dataset joins the corridors to the FLown stocks to express remittances per migrant.

Macro inflows
KNOMAD / World Bank, 2000–2025, in US dollars and as a share of GDP, 28 LAC economies plus regional aggregates.
Bilateral corridors
Central banks (Banxico, Banrep, BCB, BCE, BOJ, BRH, among others): 37,215 rows, 15 recipients, mixed monthly, quarterly and annual periodicity, with a source URL and a confidence flag per row. Complete series for Mexico, Colombia, Jamaica, Haiti, Bolivia and Suriname; thin for El Salvador, Panama, Nicaragua, Honduras and Paraguay; Guatemala and the Dominican Republic absent (national totals for Guatemala, El Salvador and Honduras are added by assumption as US corridors).
Sending costs
Remittance Prices Worldwide, 2011–2025 Q1, cost of sending 200 and 500 dollars by firm and corridor.
Derived dataset
remittances_migrant_stock: corridor-year remittances joined to FLown stocks, with completeness flags and a data dictionary; remittances per migrant and per employed migrant feed the Age Shift deck.
Caveats
Sender labels are unnormalized (the United States appears under five names); join keys run in opposite directions between the two sources; the assumed-basis corridors run about 8% high.

Profile

Population and macro context

UN World Population Prospects 2024
Population by five-year age group and sex, estimates and projection variants; used for denominators, ageing shares and dependency ratios (also World Bank WDI).
IMF exchange rates
Monthly bilateral and effective exchange rates for LAC currencies, 2010 onwards, for remittance deflation.
UNCTAD FDI and OECD DAC aid
Annual inflows by country and region, for the macro chapter of the remittances work.
Bahamas work permits
Permits by industry, occupation and nationality, 2018–2024, parsed from the Department of Immigration's PDF reports.

Questions it answers

  • How many migrants live in each LAC country and where each diaspora lives, in levels and shares, 1990 to 2024.
  • Which corridors moved in a given year (FLown), and how the intra-regional stock quadrupled from 3.5 to 13.3 million.
  • How large the forced-displacement component is, by origin and host.
  • How much money comes back, from which countries, at what cost, and per migrant.
  • Eligibility-type questions for the IDB's migration facility: inflows as a share of the population over three years.

Known traps

Two FLown files, two answers

The Hugging Face copy and the LSE estimates file differ. Every script uses the LSE file; keep it that way.

1 January vs mid-year

FLown stocks are dated 1 January and UN DESA's at mid-year; fast-growing corridors sit below UN DESA (IDB26-born in Colombia 2024: 2.74 vs 2.97 million).

Do not use FLown implied populations

The sum of all stocks into a country is off the WPP population by up to 15%. Pair FLown with WPP denominators.

LAC is not IDB26

UN DESA's Latin America and the Caribbean row includes Cuba, Puerto Rico and territories; summing the 26 IDB borrowers gives 95% of it.

No 2024 age data

Age breakdowns stop at the 2020 revision; our 2024 school-age figures are the 2020 values scaled.

Hand-edited canonical tables

The destination and origin workbooks carry manual columns; rerunning the processing script overwrites them.
